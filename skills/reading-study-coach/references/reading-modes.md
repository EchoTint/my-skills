# Reading Modes

Use this reference to choose the current reading task and preserve the quality of
textual analysis. A reading mode is not a book type or a mastery status.

## Route By The User's Request

| Request | Mode and reference | First useful result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start/resume a book, read together, learn systematically | Study: [study-workflow.md](study-workflow.md) | Restore or establish a goal-aligned route and current learning unit |
| Practice, drill, or apply an idea | [adaptive-routing.md](adaptive-routing.md) + [practice-loop.md](practice-loop.md) | Choose a ready target task, not automatically the first/next chapter |
| Only wants the gist, a summary, or a short overview | Summary | Answer at the requested length and scope |
| Look at this, decide whether to read, choose a route | Quick preview | Provisional orientation and a reading decision |
| Explain concepts, propositions, structure, or arguments | Analysis, scoped to the requested question | Source-grounded interpretation before evaluation |
| Formal analytical reading, full deep breakdown, or 15 rules | Full analysis below | Three stages retaining all 15 rules |
| Compare authors/books around a question or study a topic | Topic: [topic-study.md](topic-study.md) | Shared question and source scope |
| Read or critique a research paper | Paper: [paper-reading.md](paper-reading.md) | Paper-specific reading route |
| Ingest, query, review, questions, or status | Preserve the explicit command | Do not restart a book or replace the command with a report |

- A systematic-learning request about a technical book defaults to Study, not a
  passive technical summary. The same applies to other book types.
- Any book can receive a gist-only answer. Do not impose tests, a new wiki, or a
  complete learning plan on an explicit summary request.
- An unclear reading request starts with a small useful preview. State the
  assumption and proceed; ask one question only if ambiguity changes the route.
- When a study round needs close textual interpretation, use the analysis
  procedures inside that round. Assistant analysis does not prove user mastery.
- A learner's reordered route does not change the author's textual structure.
  Analytical reconstruction must preserve the source's actual order/relations.
- Select the type adapter from [book-types.md](book-types.md). The user's goal
  determines depth; the type determines relevant questions and evidence.
- Source availability and intake are governed by
  [source-intake.md](source-intake.md). Match claims to the actually read scope.

## Quick Preview

Inspection is orientation, not a completed analytical reading.

1. Inspect accessible title/edition information, contents, preface, headings,
   opening/closing passages, figures, and a purposeful sample as available.
2. State what the text appears to be, its visible organization, likely audience,
   relevance to the user's question, and the evidence used.
3. Recommend close reading, selective reading, skimming, reference use, or not
   continuing. Explain the decision rather than filling every possible field.
4. Identify questions or passages that need deeper reading.

Answer the four active-reading questions provisionally:

- Whole: What does the text appear to be about?
- Details/how: What ideas and forms of presentation are visible?
- Soundness: What needs checking before a judgment is possible?
- Relevance: Why might this matter to this reader?

Use language such as "based on the contents," "appears to," and "needs source
confirmation." Unknown details remain unknown. Do not present sampled reading
as whole-book coverage, reconstruct a definitive argument, or deliver a formal
truth judgment in this mode. A visible risk is a question to check, not proof.

Default output: brief orientation, reading decision, evidence/limitations, and
one next step. Use an extensive inspection report only when it serves the request.

## Summary

- Summarize the requested source and range at the requested length.
- Start with the gist; add only the main points needed for the user's purpose.
- Distinguish what the source says from interpretation or personal application.
- Do not turn "summarize" into a compulsory full analysis or a mastery test.
- If the request also asks for evidence, concepts, logic, or critique, add the
  relevant analysis rather than treating compression as proof of understanding.
- Respect the literary spoiler boundary in [book-types.md](book-types.md).

## Full Analytical Reading

Use all three stages and all 15 rules for an explicitly formal analytical
reading. For a narrow concept or argument question, apply the relevant rules to
that scope without pretending the whole book has been analyzed.

Keep the four questions visible throughout: what the whole is about, what is
said and how, whether it holds up, and what follows for the reader. Adapt their
meaning to the genre; fiction is not an empirical treatise.

### Stage 1: Establish The Whole And Its Problem

1. **Classify with evidence.** Identify form, function, and specific type; explain
   the reading consequence. Select primary/secondary adapters from
   [book-types.md](book-types.md), and mark unresolved classification.
2. **State the whole precisely.** Give a concise, testable statement containing
   object, central question, main answer/direction, and development path. It must
   be more than a title paraphrase or a list of themes.
3. **Reconstruct the structure.** Load
   [analytical-outline.md](analytical-outline.md) before producing this rule.
   Identify the governing question, movement of the whole, functional major
   parts, chapter functions, transitions, and how the outline was constructed.
   A copied contents list or consecutive chapter summaries are not a structural
   outline. Explain what each part does and why its placement matters.
4. **Find the author's problem.** Build the main/secondary question tree, connect
   it to parts of the book, and distinguish explicit questions from inference.

Stage 1 gate: the classification changes the reading; the whole statement could
be disproved by rereading; the structure explains relations, not only topics;
the question tree is connected to actual text. Separate confirmed evidence,
reasoned inference, and passages still requiring verification.

### Stage 2: Interpret Units Before Synthesizing The Whole

Map the chapters/sections actually read to the Stage 1 questions. State what was
read closely, skimmed, or deferred. Do not flatten a long book into one claim.

5. **Reach shared meanings.** Track local key words as well as whole-book terms,
   the author's usage, and changes across reading units. Use motifs/voices when
   the literary adapter calls for them instead of inventing technical terms.
6. **Find important sentences and propositions.** Locate important sentences or
   passages, explain their role, split multiple claims, and restate them in the
   reader's own words. Preserve reliable chapter/section/page anchors.
7. **Reconstruct arguments.** For each major local argument, distinguish claim,
   reasons, evidence/examples, assumptions, inference, and conclusion. Give
   stable labels when helpful, connect arguments across units, then synthesize
   the whole-book movement. A chapter summary is not an argument reconstruction.
8. **Identify answers and remaining problems.** Return to the Stage 1 question
   tree: answered, partly answered, unanswered, or deliberately left open. State
   whether the author acknowledges each gap. This is interpretation, not yet
   a verdict about truth or adequacy.

Stage 2 gate: important units are represented or explicitly outside scope;
meanings and claims have anchors; local reasoning leads to the whole rather
than being replaced by a generic thesis; answers reconnect to the problem tree.

### Stage 3: Understand Before Criticizing

9. **Demonstrate understanding first.** Fairly state the author's overall
   position and strongest central reasoning. Identify unread or uncertain parts
   before claiming the right to judge them.
10. **Disagree without contentiousness.** Address the strongest relevant version,
    not a caricature; separate a disagreement from dislike or unmet expectations.
11. **Give reasons and distinguish knowledge from opinion.** Judge by scope:
    agree, partly agree, disagree, or suspend judgment. Tie each judgment to a
    specific proposition, argument, literary feature, or unresolved question.
12. **Check missing relevant knowledge.** Explain what information is absent,
    why it matters, and whether it would change the conclusion. Do not invent a
    defect merely to fill this rule.
13. **Check misinformation.** Identify the precise factual/premise error and
    reliable counterevidence; distinguish error from dated knowledge or dispute.
14. **Check reasoning.** Identify the exact broken inference, contradiction,
    meaning shift, unsupported generalization, or missing bridge, and its effect.
15. **Check incompleteness.** Identify an unaddressed problem, omitted relation,
    scope limit, or insufficient justification, and explain its importance.

Stage 3 gate: criticism has a target and grounds; source claims, reconstruction,
user judgment, and preference remain distinct. Say "not established" or suspend
judgment when evidence is insufficient. Say no defect was found when appropriate;
do not manufacture criticism. Apply genre-specific standards rather than judging
a poem for lacking scientific proof or a manual for lacking narrative elegance.

Finish by answering what holds up, what remains uncertain, what it means for the
user, and the next useful reading or practice step.

## Report Shape And Learning Handoffs

- Preserve all 15 rules in explicitly formal analysis; use concise grouped
  sections/tables, not empty boilerplate. Stage a long-book analysis across
  reading units or sessions rather than claiming instant complete coverage.
- Do not dump the full report during every preview, summary, query, or study turn.
- For Study, return to [study-workflow.md](study-workflow.md): user reconstruction,
  rigorous testing, required practice, and scheduled review remain intact.
- For application, transfer, and remediation, load
  [practice-loop.md](practice-loop.md); do not replace them with passive advice.
- For multi-source synthesis, use [topic-study.md](topic-study.md), not a bundle
  of independent book summaries. For papers, use [paper-reading.md](paper-reading.md).
- Persistence and mastery evidence follow [wiki-state.md](wiki-state.md); an
  impressive assistant report never upgrades the user's mastery by itself.
