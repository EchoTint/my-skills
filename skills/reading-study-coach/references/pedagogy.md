# Pedagogy: Questioning, Evidence, and Review

Use alongside [study-workflow.md](study-workflow.md). Follow the user's language
for questions and feedback. Use [wiki-state.md](wiki-state.md) for persisted
evidence, canonical goals, review history, and retention/proficiency state.

The aim is explanation, example, discrimination, and usable application, not
recognition of a clear passage. Retrieval, feedback, interleaving, mastery
learning, and spaced practice are useful techniques, not guarantees of a fixed
effect size. Do not present a universal two-standard-deviation improvement,
fixed interleaving gain, or fixed percentage forgotten after a day or week.
Results depend on the learner, material, task, and conditions.

## Teaching Is Not a Formal Test

Questioning should expose reasoning, not withhold every explanation. In normal
learning and paper-node discussion, answer the learner's questions first when
they need clarification. Teach, demonstrate, and provide hints when useful. A
beginner may need a small worked example before attempting a reconstruction.

During a formal independent test:

- Ask 1-2 questions per round, wait for the learner, and do not reveal the
  answer or supply content hints before collecting their attempt.
- A clarification that changes no content can be neutral; a hint supplying a
  principle, step, example solution, or decisive reasoning is assistance.
  Record assistance rather than disguising it as question rephrasing.
- If help is requested or needed, pause the independent attempt and switch to
  teaching. Assisted correctness is progress, not a qualifying independent pass.
- After repair, obtain the learner's restatement and use a new equivalent
  problem without solution-producing help. Do not grade answer repetition as
  independent success.

Open-book versus closed-book conditions must be explicit and recorded. A
declared source lookup can test documented-use skill; it does not prove unaided
recall. For a memory/retrieval goal, assess without the answer-bearing source.

### Question Techniques

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Clarification | Expose the learner's model | Explain that in different words. |
| Assumption probe | Identify hidden conditions | What are you assuming here? |
| Evidence request | Ground a claim | Which passage or result supports that? |
| Counterexample | Test a boundary | When would this conclusion fail? |
| Application | Transfer to a new case | What would you do in this changed scenario, and why? |
| Comparison | Discriminate adjacent ideas | Why use this concept rather than the similar one? |

In coaching, respond to the demonstrated gap rather than reflexively asking a
harder question. Correct but shallow answers need a reason; partial answers need
focused repair; repeated errors need a smaller prerequisite or a different
explanation. Do not treat slow reasoning or lack of prior exposure as stupidity.

### Hint Escalation in Coaching

Use the least help that makes progress, not an inflexible ladder:

1. Rephrase or isolate the task.
2. Ask a simpler prerequisite question.
3. Offer a concrete case to reason from.
4. Explain the relevant principle.
5. Work a minimal example, leaving meaningful steps to the learner.

After help, ask what changed in the learner's model. If independent mastery is
the next decision, verify it on an unseen equivalent problem.

## Interleaving

Every 3-4 questions in a sustained study test, mix an appropriate previously
studied concept with the current one. Embed it naturally rather than announcing
a separate review ritual. It should require both ideas, not merely append an
unrelated trivia question. Do not force a nonexistent old concept into a new
learner's first session.

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Combine | How do the old and new concepts work together here? |
| Discriminate | Which concept applies under these conditions, and why? |
| Contrast | What looks similar, and what differs in mechanism or scope? |
| Layer | What does the new idea add to the earlier explanation? |

Favor concepts the learner confuses or needs for the current goal. An old-part
failure is evidence about that old goal: record its gap and review need, without
automatically failing unrelated new goals. Use canonical IDs to avoid duplicate
review queues when several sources discuss the same concept.

## Four-Point Mastery Rubric

Do not score on confidence, eloquence, answer length, or agreement with the
assistant. Establish the expected evidence from the available source and valid
reasoning. Distinguish understanding the author's claim from endorsing it.

| Criterion | 1 point requires | Evidence to record |
|-----------|------------------|--------------------|
| Accurate | Factually/logically correct within the stated scope | The correct claim, steps, assumptions, or source-grounded interpretation |
| Explained | Explains why, not only what | Mechanism, derivation, or reasoned support in the learner's words |
| Applied | Handles a novel scenario | A valid use in a case not solved during teaching |
| Discriminated | Distinguishes nearby ideas or boundaries | A relevant contrast, limit, or condition for not using the idea |

Each scored question is out of 4, one point per criterion. Design a check so all
four dimensions can be elicited, using a compact scenario or short linked
prompts across rounds. Narrow warm-up or clarification prompts are not separate
four-point questions. If a dimension was not elicited, obtain evidence rather
than inventing a point or penalizing the learner for an unasked requirement.
Keep the conversational round to 1-2 questions rather than a large worksheet.

### Gate and Coverage

Passing requires all of the following:

1. Every scored question is at least 3/4.
2. Overall earned points divided by total possible points is at least 80%.
   Do not round a failing result upward. For example, 3/4 on the only question
   is 75% and does not meet the overall gate.
3. The required learning goals have relevant independent evidence; an easy
   subset cannot establish mastery of an untested model or application.
4. No unresolved critical misconception or misuse remains. Specify why the
   error is critical; do not use this as a vague veto on defensible disagreement.

Passing proceeds to required Practice, not directly to `Mastered`. See
[practice-loop.md](practice-loop.md) for its criteria and evidence rules.

Keep failures and assisted attempts in the history. Do not erase low scores to
raise the average or average an assisted answer into an independent pass. A
targeted retest can replace the failed decision with new evidence, not rewrite
the old attempt. State which still-valid checks and new checks support the
current coverage and score. If an affected prerequisite or model remains wrong,
do not carry forward an application pass that depends on it.

### Self-Assessment Before Reveal

After the learner attempts the question, ask for confidence before revealing
the evaluation or answer. Use these labels or natural equivalents:

- Solid: could explain it and handle a relevant edge case.
- Mostly there: understands the core but may struggle with changed conditions.
- Shaky: has a rough model but would not trust its application yet.
- Lost: cannot reconstruct the idea.

Compare confidence with concrete rubric evidence. High confidence and weak
evidence suggests a familiarity/fluency illusion; name the specific gap without
shaming. Low confidence with strong evidence calls for evidence-based reassurance.
High confidence and a passing test is still not the Practice gate or long-term
retention. Confidence never awards or removes a rubric point.

## Misconceptions and Prerequisite Repair

Diagnose the difference between an unknown prerequisite, a wrong model, an
omitted boundary, and an execution mistake. A learner who has never encountered
an idea needs teaching, not repeated recall demands.

Common traps include overgeneralization, literal use of a metaphor, vocabulary
confusion, correlation mistaken for causation, an incomplete model, and treating
an author's framing as universal truth.

For a wrong model, a supported counterexample is often useful:

1. Identify the learner's actual claim, without inventing a misconception.
2. Ask for its prediction in a concrete situation.
3. Compare it with a source-supported result or a justified logical consequence.
   Do not invent an empirical result to make the teaching story work.
4. Let the learner explain the mismatch; provide clarification if they are stuck.
5. Have the learner state what was wrong and reconstruct the corrected model.
6. Check a new scenario that would have triggered the old misconception,
   independently when it is part of a mastery decision.

A misconception is not resolved by agreeing with the assistant or getting the
same question right after the answer was supplied. Require both an explanation
of the old error and correct handling of a new relevant scenario. Keep source
uncertainty visible; a reasoned critique of a contested claim is not an error
merely because the author takes a different view.

## Spaced Review

Spacing is the default review policy, not a prediction of the learner's personal
forgetting curve. Preserve the exact schedule:

| Event | Next review from the actual attempt/completion date |
|-------|----------------------------------------------------|
| First independent test plus Practice pass | +1 day |
| Successful first due review | +3 days |
| Next successful review | +7 days |
| Next successful review | +14 days |
| Next successful review | +30 days |
| Next and subsequent successful reviews | +60 days |
| Failed independent review | Restart at +1 day |

These are successive intervals, not doubled durations or dates measured from
the original reading date. Persist `review_step` and the full attempt history
according to the shared schema. A failure resets the interval, not the history.
A deferred or unattempted review does not advance the step.

### Review Questions and Decisions

- Start with independent retrieval: do not show notes or the answer first for a
  recall goal. Prefer a changed application, explanation, or discrimination.
- Use one compact scored check per due goal, not an entire chapter exam. Make
  the four dimensions assessable and apply the same explicit scoring gate to
  the review set. Ask confidence before revealing feedback.
- A hint-supported correction does not justify extending the independent
  retention interval. Record it, repair the gap, and restart at 1 day.
- Use feedback to distinguish a retrieval lapse from a conceptual or practical
  failure. Repair the affected part and independently recheck it; require a new
  practice artifact if the old practice evidence no longer supports ability.
- Keep prior qualifying evidence, while updating the current review need and
  retention according to the shared schema. Do not claim permanent loss or
  permanent mastery from one attempt.

### Strong-Study Return Protocol

1. Read the plan and mastery map before advancing.
2. Identify due canonical goals; prioritize overdue items and current weak
   prerequisites. Randomize only among similarly important items if helpful.
3. Review at most five goals in the session, or fewer for the learner's budget.
4. Save results, `review_step`, and next dates. Unattempted backlog remains due.
5. Address a failed dependency before declaring a dependent new goal mastered.
   If the learner pauses, defers review, or switches to reading, preserve all
   pending work instead of silently granting a pass.

## Adaptive Pacing and Recovery

| Signal | Adjustment |
|--------|------------|
| Accurate, well-reasoned independent answers | Increase challenge or combine compatible goals without skipping required evidence |
| Correct but slow reasoning | Give time; do not penalize speed unless speed is an explicit goal |
| Partially correct | Isolate the gap; teach or ask a focused follow-up in the appropriate mode |
| Repeated failure | Check one prerequisite, break the task down, or change the explanation |
| Frustration or fatigue | Reduce workload, use a smaller case, or pause; do not lower thresholds |
| Boredom | Add a meaningful changed constraint tied to the learner's goal |
| Genuine curiosity or an unresolved question | Explain and explore first; save the formal assessment as pending |

On interruption, retain the current model, unresolved gap, assistance used, and
smallest next action. On return, restore that state and check due reviews rather
than restarting the interview or retesting everything. Let evidence and the
learner's feedback choose the next step, not a rigid obligation to keep asking.
